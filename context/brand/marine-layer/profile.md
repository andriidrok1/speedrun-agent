# Marine Layer: brand profile

> **Campaign, budget and negotiation rules are fictional for the demo.** The company facts in "Who they are" and "Products" are public and cited. Everything from "Campaign brief" onward (dates, budget, CPM, concession rules, past deals, the negotiator persona) is invented for the hackathon and does not describe Marine Layer's real marketing.

## Who they are (public, cited)

Marine Layer is a San Francisco casual-apparel brand founded in 2009 by Michael Natenshon and Adam Lynch ([Merchology](https://www.merchology.com/pages/marine-layer-partner-highlight)). The founder tried to recreate a favorite vintage tee after his girlfriend threw it out; the company grew out of that. Their line: "It all began with a really soft tee." First store on Chestnut Street, still "Born & Based in San Francisco" ([about-us](https://www.marinelayer.com/pages/about-us)).

Scale: about 55 US stores and roughly $200M revenue over the past year ([Entrepreneur, Feb 2026](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/businesses-cash-in-on-gen-zs-love-for-experiential-retail-marine-layer-abbode)). Same article: two pop-ups opened Oct 2025 (The Custom Club on Fillmore St, SF; The Patch Bar under the Nolita store, NYC) and became some of their busiest locations.

Sustainability: Certified B Corp since Dec 2022, score 81.8, with 575,000+ tees collected through Re-Spun by that date ([B Lab](https://www.bcorporation.net/en-us/find-a-b-corp/company/marine-layer/)). Re-Spun started in 2019 ([blog](https://www.marinelayer.com/blogs/blog/re-spun-recap-100-000-tees-later)); today a $20 Take Back Bag returns a $40 reward ([product page](https://www.marinelayer.com/products/ml-take-back-bag-1)). Impact page: "Celebrating 7 Years of Re-Spun" ([impact report](https://www.marinelayer.com/pages/impact-report?view=impact-report)).

Affiliate program runs on Refersion; signup asks for Instagram handle, tee size and pant size, so it is built for gifted creators ([marinelayer.refersion.com](https://marinelayer.refersion.com/)). Commission is unpublished there; one roundup lists ~12.41% via Skimlinks ([source](https://prettyprofitablemarketing.com/fashion-affiliate-programs/)), treat as approximate.

## Campaign brief (fictional)

**Fall 2026 "Re-Spun" launch.** Window Oct 15 to Nov 30, 2026. Goal: reach 18-34 year olds in SF, LA, NYC and Austin with the story that the softest tee in the closet is made from last year's tees. Wanted from each creator: 1 Instagram Reel plus 3 Stories; a TikTok cut is optional and paid as an add-on. About 10 creators total. Product must be on screen for at least 8 seconds of the Reel and the Re-Spun name has to be said or shown once.

Success: CPM at or under $25 on delivered views, tracked affiliate sales, clips the brand can repost. Paid-ads usage is optional.

## Brand voice

Warm, a little goofy, never salesy. Short sentences. Talks about softness the way other brands talk about performance. California as a mood, not a postcard. Three sentences in their register:

- "We're obsessed with softness. Like, unreasonably."
- "Your old tee didn't die. It got Re-Spun."
- "Weekend clothes for the other five days too."

## Content guidelines

- Everyday wear in real places: the walk to coffee, the ferry, a Sunday cook. No studio sets.
- Say what the fabric feels like. Let the creator use their own words; the brand does not want a script read.
- Re-Spun angle should be one line, delivered casually. No lecture on textile waste.
- Product visible at least 8 seconds in the Reel; hoodie or tee in frame, not folded on a table.
- Tag @marinelayer, use the campaign link or code. Disclose per FTC (#ad or the paid partnership label).
- Natural light preferred. Music from the platform's licensed library only.

## What a great creator looks like to them

Someone whose feed already looks like a Marine Layer store: lived-in, coastal, not luxury. 20k to 250k followers with real comments. Based in or often filming in the four campaign cities. Has worn the brand or similar brands before (they check). Willing to run an affiliate link, which tells them the creator believes the product will sell. Bonus for SF outdoor shooters, since the HQ event is there.

## How the negotiator behaves (fictional persona)

Friendly, quick, first names. Data-driven: asks for average Reel views from the last 10 posts and quotes CPM math back. Opens at 55% of the private per-creator max, framed as "here's the mix we usually start with." Prefers product-plus-cash over pure cash because product costs them about 40 cents on the retail dollar. Values affiliate because it is performance-based and self-funding; will trade cash for a higher affiliate percentage almost every time. Concedes 12% of max per round, 6 rounds, then walks. Throws in non-cash sweeteners (HQ launch-event invite, brand IG feature, early access to drops) freely; they cost almost nothing and creators like them.

## Red lines

- No comparisons to fast-fashion brands, even flattering ones.
- No discount codes above 20%.
- No political content in the deliverable or in the caption.
- No competitor apparel visible in the same Reel.
- No perpetual usage rights bought for this campaign; 90-day paid is the ceiling.
- Per-creator spend above $6,500 all-in (cash + wholesale product + credit) is an automatic reject.

## Past deals (made up, for pattern matching)

1. **Spring 2026, LA lifestyle creator, 85k IG.** 1 Reel + 3 Stories for $2,200 cash, $400 retail in product (a Corbet hoodie, two tees, a Saturday chino), 15% affiliate, 30-day paid-ads rights. Reel did 190k views (about $12 CPM on cash), affiliate drove $3,100 in tracked sales, and the creator asked to renew unprompted.
2. **Holiday 2025, SF running creator, 40k IG + 60k TikTok.** No cash. $600 retail in product, $500 store credit, 20% affiliate, invite to the Fillmore Custom Club opening, reposted on brand IG. Delivered 1 Reel, 3 Stories, 1 TikTok. Combined 140k views, best cost-per-view of the season; she still tags the brand unpaid.
